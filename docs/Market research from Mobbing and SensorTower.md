# AI coach with goal-based plans and form tracking

Who makes money in Health & Fitness, what they charge for a subscription, and how the top apps build onboarding. Numbers come from Sensor Tower, flows from Mobbin; the feature matrix extends the earlier competitor analysis.

Sensor Tower · App Store · estimates for October 2026 · Mobbin · iOS onboarding flows · Free accounts: ranges only

## Key findings

#2

**Ladder is #2 by revenue in H&F (US, iPhone)**, even though it is the most expensive workout app: $29.99–49.99/mo. People pay for a coach's face and a ready-made program, not for the number of features.

6 / 25

**Only 6 workout apps in the top 25 by revenue.** The rest are calorie counters (MyFitnessPal, Cal AI, MacroFactor, Lose It), sleep and meditation. The workout niche is narrow, so a newcomer is easy to notice.

$1–10M

**Monthly revenue for Fitbod, Ladder and Runna** at 100–500K downloads. This is our realistic benchmark. Strava (1–10M downloads, $10M+) plays in a different league as a social network.

0

**None of the top 25 by revenue uses the camera.** Zing, Kemtai and Tempo, which do, either were not found in Sensor Tower or have \<5K downloads. The market has not yet proven people pay for it, and that is exactly where the open space is.

8 steps

**The quiz standard.** Fitbod has 8 steps with a progress indicator and "Skip" on each; Runna asks about 7 questions and then shows a plan summary explaining "why this one".

7 days

**Ladder shows a trial timeline:** start today, reminder on day 5, payment on day 7. This directly addresses the main complaint about Fitbod and Zing: unexpected charges.

Prices fall into three clusters: $12–16 for a tracker or generator (JEFIT, Strava, Fitbod, Peloton One), $20–25 for a branded plan (Runna, Sweat), $30–50 for a "coach with a face" (Ladder, Tempo). A product with a camera and post-workout review can launch at $15–20 and move toward Ladder's prices once there is an AI-coach persona.

## Onboarding on Mobbin

Free Mobbin shows only onboarding flows. Workout, summary and paywall screens are behind Mobbin's own paywall. Freeletics is not in the library.

### Fitbod

39 screens · 51 flows

1. 3 value slides: "Science-backed workouts that get better as you do"
2. Lifting experience: beginner, intermediate, advanced
3. Goal: 6 options, from building muscle to tracking your own routines
4. Where you train: large gym, small gym, garage, home, no equipment
5. Equipment: a grid of cards, pre-filled based on location
6. Frequency: days per week or specific days
7. Push notification with a workout preview and time picker
8. Which muscles you trained recently, to calculate recovery
9. Body: sync with Apple Health or manual entry
10. Account: Apple or email

Strong

- Location pre-fills equipment, so the user only unchecks what they don't have
- "(n/8)" indicator and "Skip" on every step
- Muscle recovery is accounted for from day one

Weak

- Long flow: equipment alone takes 4–5 screens
- The error "Insufficient info for calories burned" appears before any data is entered

### Ladder

14 screens · 78 flows

1. Sign in with Apple on the first screen: "Free to try · No credit card"
2. Coach video: "Ladder helps you train with purpose"
3. "Let's find you a team": a coach's team instead of a personal plan
4. Filter: style (up to 3), equipment, length 30/45/60+, coach gender
5. Team cards: coach, description, "Learn more" and "Try team" buttons
6. Trial timeline: start, Welcome Workout, reminder on day 5, payment on day 7
7. "Trial started", "Yes, remind me" button
8. Home screen: Welcome Workout and a challenge "complete a workout to unlock your plan"

Strong

- A transparent trial removes the fear of being charged
- The coach's face and video create the feeling of a live coach
- First value within 2 minutes: Welcome Workout

Weak

- An account is required before the person has seen any value
- The plan is not personal: you pick a team rather than get a program built for you

### Runna

25 screens · 170 flows

1. "Your new running coach. Any distance. Any goal."
2. Sign-up: email, Apple, Google or Strava, then a "Welcome to the club" greeting and a referral code
3. Goal: race, distance, start running, improve 5K, parkrun, postnatal, functional fitness
4. Recommended plan (carousel): "New To Running, 8 weeks, 5 km"
5. Current activity: 3 levels
6. How many days per week and which ones
7. "What else are you interested in": strength, mobility, injuries, nutrition, sleep
8. Push priming: "with reminders you're 2x more likely to finish your plan"
9. "Your plan is almost ready" with an explanation of why it looks this way, then generation

Strong

- Plan explanation: "because you can run on Sat and Sun"
- Push priming is backed by a concrete number
- The "what else interests you" step shows demand for several goals at once, including strength

Weak

- Sign-up comes before any question about the goal
- Strength appears only as an "interest", with no dedicated program

## Market gaps

### Table stakes

- Quiz of up to 8 steps with a progress indicator and "Skip"
- Personal plan that adapts after workouts
- Equipment based on location
- Exercise videos and a set log
- Reminders on workout days
- Apple Health

### Open space

- **Camera and post-workout review.** Nobody in the top 25 by revenue has a camera, and those who do give cues only during the movement
- **Several goals in one plan.** Runna already asks about strength, Ladder has hybrid teams, but nobody builds a single personal plan
- **An honest trial.** A timeline like Ladder's, but without an account on the first screen

### Not needed at launch

- Teams and live coaches (Ladder, Future)
- Nutrition: Ladder has 6 food-logging flows, and it is a separate market
- Social network and challenges (Strava)
- Own hardware (Tempo)
- Live Activities and widgets

## MVP scope

### Must-have for the hackathon

1. A 6–8 step quiz with no sign-up: goal (several allowed), level, location with equipment, days
2. A "your plan" screen with an explanation, like Runna's
3. Camera-tracked workout for squats, push-ups and lunges: rep counter and 2–3 form rules (MediaPipe or MoveNet in the browser)
4. AI post-workout review: 3 tips and changes to the next workout
5. Paywall with a trial timeline

### Nice-to-have for v1.1

- Chat with an AI coach that has a persona, face and voice
- Voice cues during a set
- Push priming with a number
- Apple Health and Strava
- Running blocks in the plan

### Postpone

- Nutrition and barcode scanner
- Teams, community, live coaches
- Native iOS and Android (PWA at launch)
- Hardware, B2B
